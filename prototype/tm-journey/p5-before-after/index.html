Act 2 · What this is

What exists today, and what we are designing

TokenMax is one use case of Jev, the question-answering tool this site is named after. Three parts are built today. The pacing tool is a design.

Built today

The map

A map of your own last 7 days, made on your computer from your Claude Code and Codex history. It writes a private report and a share draft into a new folder, and shows:

  • sessions in Claude Code and in Codex
  • limit hits, and whether each was the short window, the weekly one, or unknown
  • output from each tool
  • how much of Claude's output came from the big model
  • how much of the Codex week is used
  • up to three proposals, each labelled a proposal, not measured savings
Built today

The share link and the board

The map gives you a link that holds your week's totals and nothing else. If you open it and press Share, those totals join a public board of maps, listed newest first, self-reported, not verified.

Design, not built

The pacing tool

The idea: send each job to the cheaper door that can do it, with Jev picking the door for the jobs a fixed checklist cannot place, so the week's allowance is paced and used rather than hit. Pace it, use it all, not run out.

In rehearsal, not run yet. Nothing on this page is a result from it.

Act 3 · Decide to try

What you give, and what you keep

  • Made on your own computer. It reads your Claude Code and Codex history there and leaves those files unchanged.
  • Sends nothing by itself. It writes your map into a new folder and gives you a link.
  • Sharing is a separate choice. Open the link, check the fields, press Share. Only the week's totals go: sessions, limit hits by window, output per tool, the big-model share, the Codex week used. No file paths, project names, ids or timestamps.
  • Your last 7 days. A longer period only if you ask for it.
  • It will not tell you why you hit a limit, or what your week cost. It counts; the report says token counts are not subscription percentages or cost.
  • Why share at all. Weeks shared by willing people help decide whether the router Design, not built is worth building.

Act 4 · Make your map

One line, and nothing is sent

  • What it does: reads your Claude Code and Codex history and writes your map into a new folder.
  • Where it runs: on your own computer. You paste one line into a terminal (on a Mac, the Terminal app) and press Enter.
  • What leaves: nothing, until you open the link it prints and press Share there.
  • What is shared then: only the week's totals, the same fields as the sample card.

For the example week, the end of what it printed looked like this:

wrote <your new folder>/private-report.md wrote <your new folder>/share-draft.md (scan: clean) claude sessions 515, codex sessions 3478, cap hits 2 Share fields (tm-share/1). Nothing has been sent. ... Open this link to review and share: https://jevnotjev.breakoutwithai.com/maps/#m=eyJ2IjoidG0tc2hhcmUvMSIs...
It says bun is not found, or I want more than a week

Bun is the program that runs the map. If it is missing, install it with this line, then paste the map line again: curl -fsSL https://bun.sh/install | bash

For a longer period, add --days 30 to the end of the map line (up to 90 days).

Paste this line and press Enter:

curl -fsSL https://jevnotjev.breakoutwithai.com/tm/map.ts | bun run - --share

Proposals (not measured savings)

  1. 2 limit hits in the week: put must-deliver work early in the window and queue optional work for after the reset.
  2. 1 hit has no named window: the live TokenMax band can tell the five-hour window from the weekly one when the limit fires.
  3. 87% of Claude output came from Opus-class models: test whether routine helper work holds up on a smaller model before moving it.
